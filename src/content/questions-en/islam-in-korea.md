Many people think of Islam as an unfamiliar religion that arrived recently, but the bond between Korea and Islam runs deeper than you might expect.

## Muslim merchants in Silla and Goryeo

9th-century Arab geographers recorded a country called "Silla" at the far eastern edge of the world. In the Goryeo period, Muslim merchants known as *Hoehoe-in* (回回人) lived in the capital, Gaegyeong, and a widely known interpretation holds that the "Hoehoe-abi" in the Goryeo song *Ssanghwajeom* refers to them.

## The Korean War and the Turkish soldiers

Modern Islam in Korea began with the Korean War. The Turkish Brigade, part of the UN forces, sent close to 15,000 soldiers and suffered heavy losses defending Korea. Even during the war, they set up an orphanage and cared for war orphans.

Imams (religious leaders) in the Turkish units introduced Islam to Koreans, which led to the founding of the Korea Islamic Society in 1955. It later became the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF) in 1967.

## Seoul Central Mosque

In 1976, Seoul Central Mosque opened on a hill in Itaewon, Yongsan-gu, Seoul. Korea's first mosque, it is still the centre of the Korean Muslim community. Mosques followed in Busan, Jeonju, Gwangju (Gyeonggi) and other cities, and today there are around twenty mosques and many prayer rooms across the country.

## Muslims in Korea today

The number of Muslims in Korea is estimated at 150,000 to 250,000. Most are people who came from Indonesia, Uzbekistan, Pakistan, Bangladesh and elsewhere to work or study, and there are reported to be tens of thousands of Korean Muslims. Seoul Central Mosque says a good number of young Koreans visit every month wanting to learn about Islam.
