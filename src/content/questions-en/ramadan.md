"You go hungry for a whole month?" Many people are surprised when they hear about Ramadan. In fact, Muslims don't fast all month long: they fast only during the day, and after sunset they eat with family and neighbours.

## When is it?

Islam uses a lunar calendar, so Ramadan moves about 11 days earlier each year in the solar calendar. When it falls in summer, the days are long and so are the fasting hours; in winter, they're short.

## How is it done?

- Before dawn, people eat a light meal called *suhur*.
- While the sun is up, they abstain from food, water and smoking. They also take extra care to avoid anger, gossip and lies.
- At sunset they break the fast with dates and water, and share an evening meal called *iftar*.
- At night, many gather at the mosque to read the Qur'an and pray.

Children, the elderly, the sick, pregnant women and travellers, among others, don't have to fast (2:184-185). It's not meant to be done at the cost of one's health.

## Why do it?

> "O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may become mindful of God." (Qur'an 2:183)

For Muslims, Ramadan is a time of:

- **Restraint and practice**: enduring hunger to master desire. It resembles Buddhist practice and the Catholic Lenten fast.
- **Empathy and sharing**: feeling in one's own body what hungry people feel, and giving especially generously this month.
- **A fresh start**: a month to look at the habits of body and mind and renew them.

When Ramadan ends, Muslims celebrate a big festival called *Eid al-Fitr*. Like Korean New Year (Seollal), people put on new clothes and visit family and neighbours. Mosques like Seoul Central Mosque sometimes invite neighbours to iftar, so if you get the chance, join one.
