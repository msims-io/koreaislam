Online you often see the worry that "if Muslims increase, Korea will end up under Islamic law". It spreads along with exaggerated stories about some European countries. We don't take that anxiety lightly; let's explain calmly.

## What is sharia?

In Arabic, sharia means "the path to the water". It refers to the whole way in which Muslims live according to God's will, and most of it is about things like:

- How to pray and fast
- What to eat and drink
- How to honour parents and treat neighbours
- Trading honestly and keeping promises

The punishments that appear in the news are a very small part of sharia, and even those presuppose strict conditions and the legal system of an Islamic state; they are not something individuals or groups can carry out on their own.

## Keeping the law of the land is also part of faith

> "O you who believe, fulfil your contracts." (Qur'an 5:1)

Islamic scholars see a Muslim's living in another country as a promise to keep that country's laws. So for Muslims, obeying Korean law is also a religious duty. Korean Muslims pay taxes, do military service and marry under Korean law as Korean citizens.

## Compulsion is not Islam's way

> "There is no compulsion in religion." (Qur'an 2:256)
> "To you your religion, and to me mine." (Qur'an 109:6)

Muslims make up less than 0.5% of Korea's population, and most are foreigners who have come to work or study. What Korean Muslims want is not to change Korean society, but to be accepted as good neighbours and keep their faith quietly.
