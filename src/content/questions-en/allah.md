Hearing the name "Allah", many people picture a strange foreign god. In fact, that isn't the case.

## "Allah" means "God", not a separate name

The Arabic word *Allah* refers to the one Creator, just like "God" in English or "하나님" in Korean. That's why Arab Christians in Egypt or Lebanon also call God "Allah" in worship and prayer. The first verse of the Arabic Bible reads, "In the beginning Allah created the heavens and the earth."

On this site we write "하나님" (God), which reads naturally to Korean readers.

## The God Muslims believe in

One short chapter of the Qur'an sums up how Muslims understand God:

> "Say: He is God, the One. God, the Eternal. He neither begets nor is begotten, and there is none comparable to Him." (Qur'an 112)

Almost every chapter of the Qur'an begins with "In the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate." For Muslims, God is above all **a God of mercy**.

## Is He the same God as in Christianity?

Muslims believe so. The Qur'an teaches Muslims to say to Jews and Christians: "Our God and your God is one." (Qur'an 29:46)

There are differences in how God is understood, though. Christianity understands God as the Trinity of Father, Son and Holy Spirit; Islam believes God is strictly one, and that Jesus is a great prophet, not God. We explain this in more detail in [Do Muslims believe in Jesus?](/en/questions/jesus/)
