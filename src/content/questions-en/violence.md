For many Koreans, the first time they heard about Islam was through news of terrorism. The deaths of Kim Sun-il in Iraq in 2004, and of Korean volunteers in Afghanistan in 2007, left a deep wound in Korea. That pain and fear are completely understandable, and Korean Muslims grieve those events too.

So does Islam really teach violence?

## What the Qur'an says about life

> "Whoever kills one innocent person, it is as if he has killed all of humanity; and whoever saves one life, it is as if he has saved all of humanity." (meaning of Qur'an 5:32)

In Islam, harming innocent people, especially women, children, the elderly and people of religion, is a grave sin, even in wartime. The Qur'an teaches that those who do not attack you should be treated "with kindness and justice" (60:8).

## Then what is "jihad"?

Jihad does not mean "holy war". It means "striving" or "effort". Many Muslims regard the struggle against their own greed, laziness and anger as the greatest jihad. The Qur'an permits the use of force only in self-defence when attacked, and even then with the condition "do not transgress limits" (2:190). It is not something individuals or armed groups can declare at will.

## What about terrorist groups?

Groups such as ISIS and al-Qaeda borrow the name of religion to pursue political and violent goals. In 2014, more than 120 Islamic scholars from around the world wrote an open letter to the leader of ISIS, refuting point by point how each of their actions went against Islamic teaching.

And the people most often killed by these groups are Muslims. The countries hit hardest by terrorism are Muslim-majority countries such as Afghanistan, Iraq, Somalia and Nigeria.

## Muslims in Korea

Muslims living in Korea are ordinary neighbours who keep Korean law, go to work, study and raise families. Just as one person's crime doesn't represent their whole religion, extremists do not represent 1.9 billion Muslims.

If you still have questions, the best way is to meet Muslims and ask them yourself.
