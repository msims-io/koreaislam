When you see a woman wearing a hijab, you might wonder whether she is being made to wear it, or whether it feels stifling. That's especially so when the news often reports countries that force women to cover. That worry comes from caring about women's freedom, and Muslim women share that feeling.

## What is the hijab?

*Hijab* means "covering" in Arabic. It usually refers to a scarf covering the hair and neck. The niqab, which covers the face, and the burqa, which covers the whole body, are local customs in some regions; most Muslim women around the world either wear a hijab that leaves the face visible, or don't cover at all.

## Why wear it?

The Qur'an first tells **men** to "lower their gaze and be modest" (24:30), then tells women too to dress modestly (24:31). For Muslim women, the hijab means:

- **An expression of faith**: a personal decision to follow God's teaching.
- **"Treat me as a person, not by my looks"**: wanting to be judged by thoughts and character rather than appearance.
- **Identity**: a quiet way of saying "I am Muslim".

It may help to think of a Catholic nun wearing a veil, or a Buddhist monk shaving their head and wearing robes.

## What about countries that force it?

The Qur'an says, "There is no compulsion in religion" (2:256). Many Muslims believe a hijab worn under force has no religious meaning. When a country forces women to wear it by law, that is a political choice by its government, and many voices within that country, and among Muslims worldwide, oppose it.

Among Muslim women living in Korea, some wear the hijab and some don't. It is each person's choice.
