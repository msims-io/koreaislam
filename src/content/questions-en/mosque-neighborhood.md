In recent years, conflicts over mosque construction in places like Daegu have made the news, and more people worry about what would happen if a mosque opened near them. Worrying about changes near your home is natural for anyone.

## What is a mosque?

A mosque (*masjid* in Arabic, also called *seongwon* in Korea) is where Muslims pray together. Like a church, cathedral or Buddhist temple, it is used for:

- The five daily prayers and the Friday congregational prayer
- Qur'an and Arabic classes for children and adults
- Community events such as weddings and funerals
- Shared evening meals during Ramadan

## Inconveniences neighbours may notice

To be honest, at Friday midday many people gather for a short time, and visitors increase during festivals. Differences in food culture can feel unfamiliar too. These are things that can happen in any neighbourhood with a place of worship, like parking around a church or events at a temple.

Many mosques are aware of this and try to keep in touch with neighbours: parking guidance at prayer times, cleaning the surrounding area, events inviting neighbours in. If something bothers you, telling the mosque directly is the quickest way to sort it out.

## Aren't mosques dangerous?

In Korea there has been a mosque in Itaewon since 1976, and since then mosques have opened in Busan, Jeonju, Ansan and many other cities. A mosque is a place for prayer, and anyone can quietly observe worship. Visiting one in person will likely ease much of the worry.

## Conversation comes first

During the conflict in Daegu, pig heads were left in front of the mosque site, and the National Human Rights Commission expressed concern, judging some banners and actions to be hate speech. Muslims want to listen to their neighbours' worries while finding a way to live together through respectful conversation.
