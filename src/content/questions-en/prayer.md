You may have seen Muslims kneeling and bowing down on the street or in an airport prayer room. For Muslims, prayer is the rhythm that holds the day together.

## The five prayers

1. **Fajr**: dawn, before sunrise
2. **Dhuhr**: just after midday
3. **Asr**: afternoon
4. **Maghrib**: just after sunset
5. **Isha**: night

Prayer times follow the position of the sun, so they shift a little with the seasons and the location.

## How do Muslims pray?

Before praying, they wash their hands, mouth, face, arms and feet with water (*wudu*). Then, facing the Kaaba in Mecca, they stand, bow from the waist and prostrate with the forehead on the ground, repeating these movements while reciting verses of the Qur'an. Putting the forehead on the ground (*sujud*) is the moment of greatest humility before God.

If you think of Buddhist prostrations, especially the full-body prostration (오체투지), you'll notice the resemblance.

## Why five times?

> "Prayer keeps one away from shameful and evil deeds." (Qur'an 29:45)

Stopping work five times a day to stand before God reminds you what matters more than money, grades or what others think. Many Korean Muslims describe prayer as "five pauses for the heart each day", and say it is a great comfort in Korea's busy, highly competitive life.

## Friday congregational prayer

At midday on Friday, Muslims gather at the mosque to hear a sermon and pray together (*Jumu'ah*). That's why mosques get busy around Friday lunchtime. At many mosques, non-Muslims can quietly observe if they get in touch in advance.
