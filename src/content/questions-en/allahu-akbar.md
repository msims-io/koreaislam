Hearing this phrase in films or the news can feel chilling, because it so often comes with scenes of violence. For Muslims, though, it means something completely different.

## Meaning

*Allahu akbar* means "God is greater (than anything)". Saying it is called *takbir*.

## When do Muslims say it?

- **In prayer**: Muslim prayer begins with these words, and they are said at each change of position. With five prayers a day, that's dozens of times daily.
- **When a baby is born**: the call to prayer (*adhan*) is whispered into a newborn's ear, and these are its first words.
- **In joy or surprise**: on hearing good news, seeing a beautiful view, or when a goal is scored in football.
- **In fear or hardship**: it is also a comfort: "God is bigger than my worries."

It's similar to a Christian saying "Hallelujah" or "Lord!", or a Buddhist saying "Namu Amitabul".

## When terrorists shout it

Using a holy phrase for a crime insults the phrase and the 1.9 billion people who cherish it. Muslims are deeply saddened that it has become a symbol of terror. Sharing its real meaning is one of the reasons this site exists.
