In Korean society, which is very alert to women's rights, Islam can easily seem like "a religion that oppresses women", especially to young women. News arrives of countries where women can't get an education or work freely. We won't deny that reality. But it's worth separating what Islam teaches from what a given society does.

## Men and women in the Qur'an

> "O people, be mindful of your Lord, who created you from a single soul, and from it created its mate." (Qur'an 4:1)

The Qur'an says men and women come from the same origin, and are rewarded equally for faith and good deeds (33:35). What gives a person worth before God is not gender but heart and conduct (49:13).

## Rights recognised in the 7th century

- **Property**: a woman owns and manages her own property. It does not become her husband's when she marries.
- **Inheritance**: women inherit from their parents and husbands (4:7).
- **Consent to marriage**: the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) taught that a woman must not be married off without her consent.
- **Education**: the teaching "seeking knowledge is a duty for every Muslim" applies to men and women alike.

The first wife of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh), Khadijah, was a successful merchant, and his wife Aisha was a leading Islamic scholar who taught many male students. Al-Qarawiyyin in Morocco, often counted among the oldest universities in the world, was founded by a Muslim woman, Fatima al-Fihri.

The Prophet (pbuh) also said: "The best of you are those who are best to their wives."

## So why is reality different?

Honestly, in some Muslim-majority countries women do face discrimination. Among its strongest critics are Muslim women in those countries. They are not asking to abandon Islam; they are saying, "give us back the rights Islam originally gave us."

A religion's teaching and the reality of societies that follow it are not always the same. Seeing that gap is the first step to understanding Islam fairly.
